No official, publicly available Nike brand-voice/tone-of-voice style guide could be located. Nike does not publish its internal messaging guidelines externally (a related page by former Nike copywriter Sara Fantl, sarafantl.com/Nike-Product-Writing-Tone-of-Voice, explicitly states the full guide cannot be shared "for confidentiality purposes"). The following is therefore a summary of the best reputable secondary analysis found, from Tonelab's brand tone-of-voice breakdown of Nike, not an Nike-authored document.

According to this analysis, Nike's defining tone words are Bold and Motivational. The brand's language is imperative, built around commands rather than descriptions, and is delivered in the present tense to create a sense of urgency and immediacy. Expression is kept literal and direct, avoiding ambiguity, hedging, or tentative phrasing.

The core of Nike's messaging is framed as a central tension between "Action vs. Excuse." Every piece of communication is said to be built around pushing the reader from hesitation toward action -- the tagline "Just Do It" is cited as the clearest distillation of this, described as a phrase that "challenges hesitation, pushing for immediate action over excuses."

Key voice principles drawn from this framework include: empowering the audience through movement and attitude rather than description; fostering urgency and personal responsibility; speaking to an audience that is "ready to act" rather than persuading the reluctant; and directly challenging excuses rather than soothing them. In practice, this is interpreted as Nike avoiding passive voice, soft qualifiers, and delayed or tentative language in favor of dynamic, present-tense directives aimed at athletes and would-be athletes alike.

No specific lists of forbidden words, taboo topics, or side-by-side good/bad copy examples were present on this page or any other source actually fetched -- those elements are not included here because they were not found on a real page, in keeping with the instruction not to invent rules.
